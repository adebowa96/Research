Global Scoping Review · Research Poster · First Author · Accepted at APHA 2026

Psychosocial Outcomes and Coping in Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder

A global scoping review of 44 studies showing that PMDD's psychosocial burden is well documented in high-income countries but barely studied in low-income settings and sub-Saharan Africa.

Venue
APHA 2026 Annual Meeting and Expo (accepted poster)
Method
Arksey & O'Malley + Levac · PRISMA-ScR
Studies
1,943 records → 44 included
Literature window
2010–2025

Ifeoluwanimi P. Shobayo (first author), Chelsea Mazonde, Fadzai G. Nyarugwe, Tahirou Diallo, Melissa Lartey, Cynthia Ilechukwu, Naa Komley Commey, Atika Adams Ali, Hildah Rotich, Robyn Anderson · Liberty University

Why this question

PMDD is a hormone-linked mood disorder, classified as a depressive disorder in DSM-5, that affects about 1.6% of women and girls (based on prospectively confirmed diagnoses), roughly 31 million worldwide. It is linked to suicidal ideation, strained relationships, and reduced quality of life. Premenstrual symptoms are common across Africa, yet it was unclear how women with PMDD cope or where the evidence comes from.

What psychosocial outcomes and coping mechanisms have been reported among women with PMDD, and how are these studies distributed geographically?

Methods

  • Framework: Arksey & O'Malley with Levac et al.'s refinements, reported with PRISMA-ScR.
  • Databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, CINAHL, APA PsycInfo, APA PsycArticles, and the Cochrane Library.
  • Eligibility: peer-reviewed empirical or qualitative studies with PMDD as the primary population and at least one psychosocial outcome or coping mechanism as a primary aim.
  • Screening: 1,943 records → 325 duplicates removed → 1,618 screened → 64 assessed → 44 included.
  • Evidence mapping: by outcome, coping mechanism, publication year, and country World Bank income group.

What the evidence covers

Psychosocial outcomes reported across the 44 studies

A study could report more than one outcome.

  • Depression was by far the most studied outcome; one nationwide cohort found a 2.6-fold higher risk of later depression after a PMDD diagnosis.
  • Coping was rarely examined: only 3 of the 44 included studies examined coping mechanisms, such as peer support shared in online PMDD communities (intervention studies were outside the review's scope).
  • Geographic gap: of the 30 studies with a known country, 20 were from high-income and 7 from upper-middle-income countries; only 3 (Bangladesh, Lebanon, Nigeria) came from low- and lower-middle-income countries, just 6.8% of all 44 included studies. The other 14 used online or multinational samples or did not report a location, and Nigeria was the only sub-Saharan African setting.
  • Growing field: 61% of studies were published in 2022–2025.

Public health implications

  • Ask about suicidal thoughts when women present with severe premenstrual mood symptoms.
  • Embed PMDD screening in sexual and reproductive, school, and university health services.
  • Train providers to reduce misdiagnosis, and connect women with peer and professional support.
  • Prioritize research in African settings, including validated screening tools in local languages.
