Surveillance Data Analysis · Research Poster

Perinatal Depression Among Virginia Women, 2016–2022

Using CDC PRAMS surveillance data to track depression before, during, and after pregnancy in Virginia, and compare depression during pregnancy with aggregate estimates from participating PRAMS sites.

Data
CDC PRAMS, Virginia, 2016–2022
Population
Women with a recent live birth
Design
Secondary descriptive trend analysis
Measures
Weighted prevalence with 95% CIs

Ifeoluwanimi P. Shobayo (first author), Chelsea R. Mazonde, Marylyn O. Oduneye, Tahirou Diallo, Fadzai G. Nyarugwe, Cynthia C. Ilechukwu

Research question

How did self-reported depression before, during, and after pregnancy change among Virginia women from 2016 to 2022, and how did Virginia compare with aggregate estimates from participating PRAMS sites?

Key findings

Indicator (Virginia)20162022Relative change
Depression in the 3 months before pregnancy10.6%15.5%+46.2%
Depression during pregnancy9.4%16.9%+79.8%
Postpartum depressive symptoms12.9%14.0%+8.5%

All three indicators were higher in 2022 than in 2016, although annual estimates fluctuated (each peaked in 2019 and dropped in 2020) and the small postpartum change (+1.1 percentage points) may be within sampling error. Depression during pregnancy rose fastest: Virginia started below the aggregate PRAMS-site estimate in 2016 (9.4% vs. 11.6%), reached 17.3% in 2019 (vs. 14.8%), fell back below it in 2020, and by 2022 matched it (16.9% vs. 16.8%). Over the period, Virginia's 79.8% relative increase (+7.5 percentage points) exceeded the 44.8% increase (+5.2 points) across the aggregate PRAMS sites.

Limitations: no reported statistical tests of trend or of Virginia-versus-aggregate differences, self-reported measures (not clinical diagnoses), a descriptive design that cannot explain why reported rates rose (increases could partly reflect greater awareness, screening, or willingness to report), and no breakdown by demographic or geographic group yet (the poster's locality map is supplemental context from Virginia Department of Health model-based small-area estimates, 2020-2021).

Public health implications

  • Consistent depression screening with referral and follow-up throughout prenatal and postpartum care.
  • Better access to maternal behavioral health services.
  • Continued PRAMS surveillance, plus analysis by population group and locality to find where the need is greatest.
